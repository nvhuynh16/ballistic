\code{Epoch} is the one time type. It stores TT internally and converts on
demand to UTC, UT1, TAI, TDB, GPS. The defining offsets are exact:
$\mathrm{TT}-\mathrm{TAI}=32.184\,\mathrm{s}$ and
$\mathrm{GPS}-\mathrm{TAI}=-19\,\mathrm{s}$; UTC trails TAI by the integer
leap-second count (currently 37 s); UT1 needs EOP. \code{Epoch} subtraction
yields \emph{seconds}.

\pitfall{Note the printed $\mathrm{TT}-\mathrm{TAI}=32.18401\ldots$ --- not
exactly $32.18400$. At a Julian Date of ${\sim}2.46\times10^{6}$ one
\code{double} ulp is ${\sim}4.7\times10^{-5}\,$s, so \emph{differencing
large JDs} cannot recover a small offset to better than that. The offsets
are exact by definition; never derive sub-millisecond time differences by
subtracting raw Julian Dates.}
