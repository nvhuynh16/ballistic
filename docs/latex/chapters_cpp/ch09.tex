Three setup mistakes that silently cost accuracy, made concrete.

\examplenote{\code{examples/cpp/ex09\_pitfalls.cpp}}

\resultfile{results/ex09_pitfalls.json}{Measured pitfall deltas + status.}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Wrong ITRF realization} --- a measured ${\sim}7\,$mm LEO
divergence over one hour (ITRF2014 vs ITRF2020). Small, but real;
use the realization that matches your reference data.
\item \textbf{Missing EOP} --- you cannot un-load EOP at runtime, so the
actionable check is \code{InitResult.eop\_loaded}. Without it UT1$-$UTC
defaults to 0 $\Rightarrow$ ${\sim}3\,$m LEO error.
\item \textbf{Stale leap seconds} --- the embedded table covers through
2025. Querying later dates without a refreshed \code{leap-seconds.list}
gives seconds of TAI/UTC error $\Rightarrow$ kilometers of position.
\end{enumerate}

\pitfall{A subtler one, surfaced while building this tutorial: returning a
\code{TrajectoryInterpolator} from a scope where its \code{Trajectory} is
destroyed is a dangling reference (crash). Keep the \code{Trajectory} alive
for the interpolator's lifetime.}
