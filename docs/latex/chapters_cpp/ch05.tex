Real sensors are noisy and you do not know the truth, so hyperparameters
must be chosen by cross-validation. \code{ea::CV\{\}} runs hv-block CV (no
truth peeking), matching the deployment pipeline. We add Gaussian noise
(POS 1.0\,m, VEL 0.25\,m/s, ACC 0.1\,m/s$^2$; fixed seeds) and run each
\{obs, rate\} cell. GN (consistent-objective regularized, 2026-07) is
default-ON and benefits POS and VEL alike; the ACC facade ignores it
(circular --- \code{gn\_status} reports the skip).

The solver is the iteratively-regularized Gauss--Newton
iteration~\eqref{eq:irgn} (\cite{bakushinskii1992,kaltenbacher2008}); the
decreasing $\lambda_k$ is the continuation that, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:basin}, keeps the iterate inside the
regularization-enlarged convergence ball. The hv-block split is
\emph{provably consistent} for this serially-dependent/chaotic data
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cv}, \cite{racine2000}) --- ordinary $K$-fold is
not. The grid spans the bias--variance-optimal $\lambda$ scaling
(\S\,Mathematical Foundations, \cite{wahba1990,craven1979}).

\pitfall{An earlier edition of this tutorial measured noisy VEL diverging
outright (${\sim}29\,$km!) with bare public-API defaults. That was real,
and it was \emph{fixed} in 2026-07: the arc-length auto-clamp removed the
line-search freeze that blew VEL/ACC up on short arcs, and
consistent-objective regularized GN is now default-ON for VEL. With
current defaults, noisy VEL recovers ${\sim}0.4$--$0.5\,$m --- no research
harness needed. The remaining honest limit is ACC at
$\sigma=0.1\,$m/s$^2$ (${\sim}15\,$m at 100\,Hz, ${\sim}7\,$m at
200\,Hz): that $\sigma$ is artificially
large for an accelerometer (realistic $10^{-6}$--$10^{-2}\,$m/s$^2$,
where sub-meter returns), and double-integrating acceleration noise costs
the most position information (see the identifiability chapter). For
scale, the no-EA baseline is ${\sim}35\,$km (Chapter 4). POS + GN +
hv-block CV remains the most robust noisy path; higher sample rate helps
every modality (200\,Hz $\ge$ 100\,Hz).}
