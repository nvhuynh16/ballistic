\code{ballistic.Epoch} mirrors the C++ type. It exposes \code{.tt\_jd},
\code{.tai\_jd}, \code{.utc\_jd}, \code{.ut1\_jd}, \code{.gps\_jd},
\code{.tdb\_jd}. Defining offsets are exact ($\mathrm{TT}-\mathrm{TAI}=
32.184$, $\mathrm{GPS}-\mathrm{TAI}=-19$); UTC trails TAI by the integer
leap count; \code{Epoch - Epoch} yields seconds.

\resultfile{results/ex02_timescale_offsets.json}{Measured offsets vs TAI.}

\pitfall{$\mathrm{TT}-\mathrm{TAI}$ prints $32.18401\ldots$, not exactly
$32.18400$: at JD ${\sim}2.46\times10^{6}$ one \code{double} ulp is
${\sim}4.7\times10^{-5}\,$s. Differencing large Julian Dates cannot recover
a small offset to better than that --- the offsets are exact by
definition; do not derive sub-ms differences this way.}
